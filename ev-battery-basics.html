EV Battery Basics 2026

Types, care, and lifespan of EV batteries—what owners should know.

See Charging Guide
Key points: Most EVs use lithium-ion batteries, which are long-lasting and require minimal maintenance. Battery health is affected by charging habits, temperature, and usage.
Aspect Details
Battery Types
  • Lithium-ion (Li-ion): Most common, high energy density, long lifespan.
  • Nickel-Metal Hydride (NiMH): Used in some hybrids, less common in modern EVs.
  • Solid-State: Next-gen, safer, higher capacity, not yet mainstream.
Capacity & Range Typical EV batteries range from 30 kWh (small cars) to 100+ kWh (premium models), providing 100–350+ miles per charge.
Lifespan 8–15 years or 100,000–300,000+ miles. Most manufacturers offer 8-year warranties. Gradual capacity loss ("degradation") is normal.
Charging
  • Level 1: Standard home outlet, slowest (8–20 hours for full charge).
  • Level 2: Home/public stations, 4–8 hours for full charge.
  • DC Fast Charging: 20–60 minutes for 80% charge (varies by car).
Care & Maintenance
  • Avoid frequent deep discharges (keep battery between 20–80% when possible).
  • Minimize exposure to extreme heat/cold.
  • Use manufacturer-recommended chargers.
  • Software updates can improve battery management.
Safety Modern EV batteries have multiple safety systems (thermal management, crash protection, fire suppression). Fires are rare but possible after severe damage.
Recycling & Sustainability Batteries are recyclable. Second-life uses (e.g., home energy storage) are growing. Regulations are increasing for responsible disposal and recycling.
Cost Battery costs have dropped 80%+ since 2010. Replacement costs vary (£3,000–£10,000+), but prices continue to fall.
